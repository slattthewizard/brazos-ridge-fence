## What Drought Does to the Clay Under Your Fence Line

Most of the ground between Waco and Valley Mills is expansive clay. Blackland Prairie soil is famous for it. When that clay is wet, it swells. When it dries out, it shrinks, and it doesn't shrink a little. It shrinks enough to open surface cracks two or three inches wide and several feet deep.

Your fence posts are set in that clay. When the soil shrinks away from a post, the post loses the friction grip that holds it plumb. Then the wire tension takes over. A barbed wire fence pulling 150 to 250 pounds of tension per strand will lean any post that's lost its grip, and it always pulls toward the corners and low spots.

The [Texas A&M AgriLife Extension Service](https://agrilifeextension.tamu.edu/) has documented how expansive clays in Central Texas move structures, foundations, and yes, fence posts, through repeated shrink and swell cycles. Foundation repair companies stay busy here for the same reason fence companies do.

Here's the part most folks miss. The damage isn't done during the drought. It's done in the cycle. Clay shrinks in August, the post shifts, then October rains swell the clay back around the post in its new leaned position. Now the lean is locked in. Three or four summers of that and a fence that was dead straight in 2020 looks like a snake track.

## The 5 Drought Fence Problems We See Most in Central Texas

After enough dry summers in this county, the failure list gets predictable. Here's what shows up on our work orders every August, roughly in order.

**1. Leaning line posts.** Wood posts set 24 to 30 inches deep lose soil grip first. T-posts actually handle drought a little better because they're driven, not set in a dug hole, but they'll still lean where the cracking is bad. We cover the full diagnosis in our guide to [leaning fence posts](/blog/leaning-fence-posts/).

**2. Sagging gates.** A gate is a lever hanging off one post. When drought loosens that hinge post, a 12 foot gate multiplies the problem. The latch end drops an inch for every small tip of the post. If you're lifting your gate to latch it, the post moved, not the gate.

**3. Failed corner and brace assemblies.** Corners carry the tension of the whole stretch. When clay shrinks off a corner post, hundreds of feet of wire tension start pulling it over. Once a corner goes, every span it anchors goes slack behind it.

**4. Slack wire and dropped stays.** Wire that was tight in spring goes loose by August because the posts holding it moved inward. Loose barbed wire is how calves learn to push through. Loose net wire is how feral hogs get a snout under the bottom.

**5. Heaved posts.** Less common but real. Repeated shrink and swell can jack a shallow post upward over several seasons, an inch or two a year, until it's barely holding.

Any one of these is annoying. Two or three together is how cattle end up on the county road at 2 a.m. If that's already happened, our line on [cattle getting out of fence](/blog/cattle-getting-out-of-fence/) covers your liability exposure, and our 24/7 emergency line covers the immediate problem.

## How to Protect Your Fence Before the Next Dry Spell

You can't stop the clay from shrinking. You can make your fence a lot less sensitive to it. Here's what actually works, in rough order of bang for buck.

**Walk your line in early summer, not late summer.** Catch a corner at a 2 degree lean and it's a brace repair. Catch it at 10 degrees and it's a rebuild. June beats September every time.

**Fix small leans before the fall rains.** Remember the lock-in cycle. A post straightened and properly tamped before the rain returns to plumb. A post left leaning gets cemented into its lean by swelling clay.

**Upgrade corners and gate posts to pipe set in concrete.** Concrete spreads the load and pipe doesn't rot, so the assembly rides out shrink and swell cycles that wreck shallow wood corners. Deeper is better in clay. We set gate and corner posts 36 to 48 inches deep here for exactly this reason.

**Don't overtighten wire in August.** Wire stretched drum-tight in dry heat gets even tighter in a cold snap and pulls harder on drought-weakened posts. Snug is enough. You can take up slack again in spring.

**Watch your water gaps and low crossings.** Drought drops creek levels and exposes fence that's about to take the full force of the first flood. Drought and flood damage are two halves of the same Texas year.

**Keep an eye on official drought conditions.** The U.S. Drought Monitor at [drought.gov](https://www.drought.gov/) updates weekly and shows exactly how deep the dry spell is in McLennan, Bosque, and Falls counties. When your area slides into D2 or worse, that's your cue to walk the fence line.

## When to Stop DIY and Call a Fence Professional

Plenty of drought fixes are honest weekend work. Restapling wire, driving a replacement T-post, tightening a gate's adjustment bolts. Have at it.

But know where the line is. Call a pro when:

- **A corner or H-brace is leaning.** Corners hold the stored tension of hundreds of feet of wire. Cut or release that tension wrong and it can whip. This is the single most dangerous fence repair there is.
- **Wire needs a full restretch.** Barbed wire under stretcher tension will find your forearms if a splice or tool slips. Gloves and eye protection are minimum, and experience is the real safety gear.
- **A gate post needs replacing.** Getting the depth, concrete, and hinge alignment right the first time is the difference between a 15 year fix and a 15 month one.
- **You've straightened the same post twice.** The soil grip is gone. It needs a proper reset with compacted base material or an upgrade to pipe, not a third tamping.
- **Cattle are already getting out.** That's not a project anymore, it's a liability clock ticking. In most of this county you're under stock law, and a cow on the highway is your problem in every sense.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why do fence posts lean during a drought?

Central Texas clay shrinks as it dries, and it pulls away from the posts set in it. Once the soil loses its grip, wire tension and gate weight tip the post out of plumb. When fall rain swells the clay back, it locks the post into its new leaned position, which is why leans get worse every cycle instead of fixing themselves.

### Should I water the ground around my fence posts in a drought?

Around a high-value gate or corner post, slow soaking the soil once or twice a month can reduce cracking and help the post keep its grip, the same trick homeowners use on slab foundations. It's not practical for a mile of line fence, and it's no substitute for well-set posts. Spend the effort on the handful of posts that carry the most load.

### Will my drought-damaged fence fix itself when the rain comes back?

Mostly no. Swelling clay can firm up around a post, but it firms up around the post wherever it sits, lean and all. Slack wire stays slack and sagged gates stay sagged. The best window to straighten and reset posts is after a dry spell but before the ground fully re-swells, so the repair gets locked in instead of the damage.
