# Donkey Fence: What Holds Pet Donkeys and Guard Donkeys in Central Texas

A good donkey fence is small-mesh woven wire with a charged top wire, on posts set to take a leaning animal. Use 2 by 4 inch mesh so a hoof can't go through, run it at least 4 feet tall, and keep barbed wire out of the build. If the donkey is there to guard goats or sheep, the electric top wire needs to sit at 36 inches or higher, and the fence still has to hold the flock.

Donkeys are not horses and they are not goats. They lean, they rub, and they chew. A fence that holds a quiet horse can lose its posts to a bored donkey in a season. We build for that.

## What a donkey does to a fence

Most of the fence trouble with donkeys comes from habits, not from escape attempts.

- **Leaning and scratching.** A donkey will back up to a fence and scratch its rump on it for an hour. Loose staples, sagging net wire and leaning posts follow.
- **Chewing.** Wood top rails, gate boards and the corner post at the feed spot all get chewed. Treated lumber doesn't make a donkey stop.
- **Reaching.** They lean over to get at the grass on the other side, and the top of the mesh bows down.
- **Pushing gates.** Latches get tested daily by an animal with time on its hands.

None of that is jumping. The fence's job is to survive the daily wear and to be safe when the animal gets a leg into it.

## Height

For horses, the UGA Extension bulletin [Fences for Horses](https://fieldreport.caes.uga.edu/publications/B1192/) calls for at least 5 feet on a perimeter pasture fence and 4.5 feet on a dividing fence. A donkey is built differently from a horse and doesn't usually treat a fence like a hurdle, so the numbers can come down a little.

Our working numbers:

- **Standard donkeys:** 4 feet of woven wire as the floor, 4.5 feet if the pasture sits next to a road or a neighbor's horses.
- **Miniature donkeys:** 4 feet still, mostly to keep dogs and coyotes from walking in and to give the top wire a sensible place to sit. A mini won't need the height, but whatever is outside might.
- **Guard donkeys with sheep or goats:** whatever the stock needs. Net wire for goats is already about 4 feet tall, and the donkey lives inside it.

Those are builder numbers, not a code. The right height depends on the animal and what's next door.

## Wire: why mesh and not barbed

### Woven wire, 2 by 4 inch

The same UGA bulletin describes diamond mesh with 2 by 4 inch openings for horse fence, and says mesh openings should be small enough to keep a hoof from going through. The same logic applies to a donkey. A 4 by 4 inch or wider opening lets a hoof in. A struggling animal with a leg through wire can hurt itself badly.

That bulletin also asks for 12.5 gauge wire as a minimum. It says welded fabric with 2 by 4 inch openings is not recommended for horse fencing because the welds fail in field conditions. For donkeys we run the same way: a knotted or hinge-joint woven product, not light welded mesh. Our pages on [net wire fence](/net-wire-fence-waco/) and the [horse fence](/horse-fence-waco/) cover the products we use.

### Why we don't run barbed wire for donkeys

Barbed wire is cheap. Five strands run $2.50 to $5.50 per foot, so $3,300 to $7,260 per quarter mile. For cattle, it's often the right answer. For a donkey that scratches on the fence and leans over it, it's the wrong one.

A donkey rubbing on a barbed top wire opens its own hide. A foal or a mini that puts a leg through the strands gets cut up trying to back out. And the strands stretch as the animal leans, so the fence gets saggy and loose and needs regular tightening. Coyote and dog pressure is also not stopped by four or five open strands.

If barbed wire is already on the place, a common fix is to build the woven wire inside it, leave the old wire as the outer line, and let the donkey live behind the mesh.

### Cable, hog panels and high-tensile

- **Smooth high-tensile wire alone** is a respect fence. It works for animals that have been trained to it and it is not what we'd use on a perimeter with a donkey that leans.
- **Cattle and hog panels** have wide openings in the lower rows. They are tough, but a hoof can find a gap. They belong in a pen, not along the bottom of a donkey pasture fence.
- **Pipe and rail** is the safest against hoof entrapment, since a rail fence is less likely to catch a hoof than wire. It costs the most: welded pipe runs $12 to $28 per foot.

## The electric wire question

Hot wire for donkeys is a "yes, as an add-on" more than a fence of its own.

### Offset hot wire

The UGA bulletin says to mount electric wire on insulators on the animal's side of the post, opposite the mesh, and to keep the top wire charged so an animal can't reach over. That's how a hot wire keeps a leaner off the mesh. Put one strand near the top, about back height, on offsets facing into the pasture. A second strand lower, around chest height, takes care of the rubbers.

The donkey learns once, and the mesh stays tight longer.

### Where hot wire alone fails

Hot wire needs power, grounding and a dry-ground path to the animal. In dry Central Texas ground that gets harder in August. And a fence that goes dead for a day because the charger tripped is a fence nobody should depend on to hold a pasture full of goats against coyotes. Build the mesh as the fence and treat the hot wire as the training wire.

For chargers, tape and grounding, our post on [electric horse fence](/blog/electric-fence-for-horses/) applies, since donkeys use the same gear.

## The guard donkey fence

### What the extension sources say about guard donkeys

A Colorado State University Extension fact sheet on [guard dogs, llamas and donkeys](https://mountainlion.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/CO-O-CSU-COOP-1995-Livestock-Series-Management-Livestock-Guard-Dogs-Llamas-and-Donkeys.pdf) (1995, reviewed 2001) lays it out:

- Donkeys appear best suited to fenced pastures under 300 acres.
- Use one jenny or one gelded jack per pasture. Intact jacks are too aggressive, and two or more donkeys might stay together instead of with the sheep.
- Plan for about 4 to 6 weeks for the donkey to bond with the flock.
- Remove the donkey during lambing, because it might trample lambs or disrupt the ewe-lamb bond.
- The survey results were mixed. The fact sheet says donkeys were rated less successful than guard dogs and llamas, and that the comparisons are inconclusive.

That last point matters. A guard donkey is a help, not a guarantee, and the fence still carries the weight. We'd read this as: don't skip the predator-proofing because you bought a donkey.

### Fence heights for a guard donkey

The USDA NRCS Arkansas fact sheet [Electric Fence for Small Ruminants](https://kerrcenter.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/02/fence_fs_small_rums.pdf) says fences need to accommodate guard animals. If donkeys or llamas are used, the top wire should be at least 36 inches high, though ideally the guards will bond with the flock and not jump into the next field. The same sheet notes that a donkey's higher water needs need to be considered.

On the Waco side, that second point turns into a fence question. If your water is a creek or a pond, the water gap has to work for a donkey as well as goats, and a flood-prone creek is not where you want a hot wire. A trough on high ground is easier to fence around.

### A mesh build that works

Say you have 20 acres outside Lorena in goats, and you're adding one gelded jack to cut down on coyote losses. A build that suits it:

1. Net wire about 4 feet tall on the perimeter, tight to the ground.
2. A 12.5 gauge top wire and, if budget allows, a charged offset strand at 36 inches or higher on the pasture side.
3. Treated or steel posts at the corners and H-braces, since a donkey that scratches on a corner will find out if it's weak.
4. A pen with a smaller fence for the first weeks, so the donkey and flock get to know each other. The CSU sheet says to introduce donkeys to sheep in small pens.

For the goat side of the fence, our posts on the [best fence for goats and sheep](/blog/fence-for-goats-and-sheep/) and [coyote proof fence](/blog/coyote-proof-fence/) cover bottom-wire and apron details that apply no matter what you put inside.

## Posts, gates and the weak spots

- **Corners and braces.** A leaning animal puts steady load on the corner assembly. A properly built H-brace matters more with a donkey than with a quiet horse.
- **Line posts.** Wood line posts hold up better than t-posts to a donkey that leans and scratches.
- **Soil.** Blackland clay heaves and leans posts. West of the Brazos, limestone makes post holes harder. Our [clay soil post](/blog/fence-posts-in-clay-soil/) covers the first problem.
- **Gates.** Use a gate that has no gap a hoof or head can pass through at the hinge side or latch side. UGA's bulletin says to avoid gaps between gates and supporting posts for the same reason. Pick a latch the donkey can't flip.
- **Gap at the ground.** A donkey doesn't dig the way a hog does, but a coyote will. Keep the mesh tight to the grade, and see the guard-donkey section above before you rely on the donkey for the whole job.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How tall should a donkey fence be?

Four feet of woven wire is a sound floor for standard donkeys, and 4.5 feet is better near a road. For guard donkeys with goats or sheep, the NRCS Arkansas fact sheet says the top wire of an electric fence should be at least 36 inches high. Beyond that, it depends on the animal and the neighbors.

### Can you use barbed wire for donkeys?

You can, but we don't. Donkeys lean, scratch and reach over, so barbed top wire cuts them and loosens fast. Woven wire with 2 by 4 inch openings is the better pick, with a hot wire offset to the inside. If barbed wire is already there, build the mesh inside it.

### Will an electric fence hold a donkey?

A trained donkey respects a charged wire, but we wouldn't use hot wire alone on a perimeter. Power outages and dry ground both weaken the shock. Use electric as an add-on to mesh: an offset strand at top and one lower keeps the donkey off the wire.

### Can one guard donkey protect a whole pasture?

Not on its own. Colorado State University Extension says to use one jenny or gelded jack per pasture, and that donkeys seem best suited to fenced pastures under 300 acres. The same sheet says guard donkeys were rated less successful than guard dogs and llamas in surveys, so keep the predator-proof fence in place.

### What size mesh should I use for a miniature donkey?

Use 2 by 4 inch woven mesh, the same opening UGA Extension describes for horse fence. It's small enough to keep a hoof from passing through, which matters even more for a mini with small feet and a habit of poking around. Avoid wide-opening welded panels along the bottom.

### Can donkeys share a pasture with horses or cattle?

On the fence side, yes: a horse-safe woven wire fence handles both. Where a donkey is guarding sheep or goats, the CSU sheet says to remove it during lambing, and to test a new donkey's response to a dog before trusting it. Check feed too, since the same sheet warns some feed additives are poisonous to donkeys.
