# Fence Building Tools: What You Need for Ranch Wire Fence, Job by Job

The short list of fence building tools for a barbed wire or net wire fence is this: a way to lay out a straight line, a way to set posts (a t-post driver, a digging bar, a post-hole digger or an auger), a way to unroll and stretch wire, a pair of fencing pliers, and heavy gloves and safety glasses. Everything else either makes the work faster or handles one special case, like rock or high-tensile wire.

Most landowners already own half of it. The usual gaps are a proper stretcher, a real digging bar and good fence pliers. The big machines (a tractor auger, a skid steer with a rock bit, a hydraulic post driver) are for renting or hiring, not buying, unless you build fence every year.

## Layout tools: getting the line straight first

A crooked line puts side load on posts, and side load pulls line posts over and loosens wire.

- **Survey pins and the plat.** On a boundary, know where the line is before anything goes in the ground. See [building a fence on the property line in Texas](/blog/fence-on-property-line-texas/).
- **A 300 ft reel tape or a measuring wheel** to measure runs and mark post spacing.
- **Stakes, flagging and marking paint** for corners, gates and every change in direction.
- **Mason line** pulled tight between corners to set the first posts to.
- **Sighting posts.** On rolling ground you cannot see one corner from the other. The University of Tennessee Extension's [Planning and Building Fences on the Farm](https://utbeef.tennessee.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/127/2020/11/PB1541.pdf) describes driving two guide posts, each set where it and both corners can be seen at once, then sighting over them and moving them until all four line up. Two flagged t-posts do the job.

Walk the line with a chainsaw before the fence tools come out. Cedar, mesquite and greenbrier will stop a wire unroller dead, and a line that has grown shut is a [fence line clearing](/fence-line-clearing-waco/) job first.

## Post tools: driving, digging and tamping

Your soil decides most of this list.

### For t-posts

- **Manual t-post driver.** A heavy capped steel tube with two handles. Slide it over the post, lift and drop; the heavier the driver, the fewer strokes. Keep it on the post on the upstroke. Lift it clear of the top and it comes down off-center, which is how people catch a hand, or the driver, in the head.
- **T-post puller.** A lever jack that pulls a post straight up. You will want one the first time a post goes in crooked.
- **Post level.** A magnetic post level reads plumb both ways at once and leaves both hands free.
- **Clip tool.** Wraps t-post clips faster than pliers do.

### For wood and pipe posts

- **Post-hole digger (clamshell).** Fine for a handful of holes in decent soil. It wears you out past a dozen.
- **Digging bar.** A 5 to 6 ft steel bar with a chisel point on one end and a tamping foot on the other. In the limestone and caliche west of the Brazos and through Bosque County, it does more work than the digger.
- **Tamping bar.** If your bar has no tamping foot, get one that does. The Texas NRCS fence specification, [Conservation Practice 382](https://leon.agrilife.org/files/2022/05/382_Fence_Specification-Revised_2015.pdf), calls for a hole at least 6 inches wider than the post, with the post centered and tamped in 4 to 6 inch lifts up to ground level.
- **Tractor PTO auger or skid steer auger.** Worth it past a few dozen holes. Rock needs a rock bit and a machine with enough down-pressure to push it; a light tractor auger just spins on caliche.
- **Hydraulic post driver.** Drives wood posts without a hole. The Tennessee guide puts a driven post at 1.7 times as strong as a tamped one. Posts larger than 4 inches may need a dull point cut on the end or an augered pilot hole first.

Blackland clay and thin soil over limestone each change the job. If your place is on rock, read [setting fence posts in rocky ground](/blog/fence-posts-in-rocky-ground/) before you rent anything. And before any auger goes in, call 811 to have buried lines marked. Power, fiber and gas lines run along roads and into homesteads, and the call is free.

## Wire tools: unrolling, stretching, tying off

### Unrolling

Dragging a barbed wire roll across a pasture by hand is how people get cut. A **wire unroller** (a spinning jenny or spinner) holds the roll on a spindle and pays it out as you walk or drive away. Some mount on a three-point hitch or a receiver hitch. For net wire, a pipe through the core of the roll does a similar job.

### Stretching

- **Barbed wire stretcher.** Grips one strand and ratchets or levers it tight against the post. The right tool for barbed wire, one strand at a time.
- **Come-along with wire grips.** Works on barbed or smooth wire if the grips clamp without biting through the galvanizing.
- **Stretcher bars for net wire.** Woven wire has to be pulled evenly across its full height or it bows. The bar clamps the full height of the roll, and chains from the top and bottom of the bar run to a come-along (or a pair of them) so the pull is even from the bottom line wire to the top.
- **Ratchet in-line strainers and a tension spring.** For high-tensile smooth wire, strainers stay in the fence so you can retension later. The Tennessee guide calls for 200 pounds per high-tensile wire, set with a tension indicator spring on one wire and the rest matched by feel.

Do not stretch barbed or smooth wire with a pickup. You cannot feel tension through a clutch, and wire that lets go under load coils back fast and unpredictably. Some crews do pull net wire with a tractor hooked to a stretcher bar, but only at a crawl, with someone watching the tension curves and the brace, and with a come-along to finish. If you have not done it before, use the come-along. To bring an old fence back to tension, see [fixing a sagging barbed wire fence](/blog/fix-sagging-barbed-wire-fence/).

### Tying off and fastening

- **Fencing pliers** (covered below), plus a framing hammer, which drives staples faster on a long run.
- **Staples and t-post clips.** The 2021 NRCS Texas spec calls for 9-gauge staples at least 1.5 inches long in any post (the 2015 edition allowed 1 inch in hardwoods such as cedar), and the University of Georgia's [Fences for the Farm](https://fieldreport.caes.uga.edu/publications/C774/fences-for-the-farm/) says never shorter than 1.5 inches, preferably 1.75 or 2. Leave the staple a hair proud so the wire can slide, and drive it diagonally across the grain so the post does not split.
- **Crimping tool and sleeves.** For splices and for tying off high-tensile wire. The NRCS spec calls for two 3/8 inch sleeves or one 3/4 inch sleeve at each splice, crimped to the maker's instructions.

## What fencing pliers actually do

If you buy one fence tool, buy this one. Fencing pliers look like a small hatchet with handles, and a decent pair does several jobs:

- **Hammer face** on one side of the head for driving staples.
- **Claw or spike** on the other side of the head for pulling staples.
- **Two wire cutters**, one on each side of the jaw.
- **Gripping jaws** for twisting tie-offs and wrapping wire around itself.
- **Splicing and crimping notches** on some models.
- **A stretching lever.** Grip the wire, set the head against the post and roll. It will not tension a fence, but it takes the slack out of a strand long enough to staple it.

Look for forged steel, and cutters hard enough for high-tensile wire if you run it. The cutter wears out first. Once it chews wire instead of cutting it, that pair goes to the barn and a new one goes in the truck.

## Bracing tools

Corners carry the whole load of a wire fence, and [H-brace fence corners](/blog/h-brace-fence-corners/) explains why they fail. The tools to build one are simple:

- **Chainsaw or handsaw** to cut the horizontal brace rail to length.
- **Drill with a long auger bit** for a steel pin. The NRCS spec lets the rail be fixed with screws, nails or a drilled steel dowel pin at least 2 inches into each post.
- **Brace wire.** Two complete loops of 9-gauge smooth wire (or one of the 12.5 gauge options in the spec) running diagonally.
- **Twist stick.** A metal rod at least 18 inches long, per the NRCS spec, used to twist the brace wire tight. It stays in place about midway along the brace wire so the wire cannot unwind.

For [pipe fence](/pipe-fence-waco/), bracing means welding: a welder, a chop saw, a grinder, a pipe notcher and a generator. That is fabrication work, and a weak corner weld fails under the first real load.

## The fence repair kit for the truck

Fence breaks at the worst hour: a limb across the wire after a storm, hogs through the bottom strand, cattle on the road. A small kit behind the seat turns a half-day job into a short one.

| Item | What it is for |
|---|---|
| Fencing pliers | Cut, splice, pull and drive staples |
| Hand wire stretcher or come-along with grips | Pull a broken strand back together |
| Splice sleeves and crimper | Fast, strong splice |
| 50 to 100 ft of barbed wire and a coil of 9-gauge smooth | Patch a gap and tie off |
| 3 or 4 t-posts and a manual driver | Replace a snapped or bent post |
| T-post clips and a pocket of staples | Refasten wire |
| Heavy leather gloves and safety glasses | Every time you touch wire under tension |
| Fence tester (electric fence only) | Find a short fast |
| Chain and a small saw | Clear a limb off the wire |
| Water and a basic first aid kit | Texas summer and barbed wire |

No sleeves? Learn the Western Union splice. The 2021 NRCS spec calls for at least 4 tight, closely spaced wraps on each side of center (the 2015 edition asked for 8, and extra wraps cost nothing), and it takes nothing but pliers. A washed-out water gap or a failed corner is past what the kit can fix, and that is a [fence repair](/fence-repair-waco/) call.

## Gloves, eyes and the rest of the safety gear

The Tennessee guide tells you to wear heavy gloves and eye protection whenever you tension wire. A strand that slips out of a stretcher or snaps at a rusted spot whips back toward whoever is standing on the line.

- **Gloves.** Heavy leather, snug enough to hold a staple. Cotton gloves catch barbs and tear.
- **Safety glasses.** Wire ends, bent staples and rock chips from a digging bar all go for the eyes.
- **Long sleeves and heavy boots** around wire, and **hearing protection** around a hydraulic driver or a rock auger.

Stretch in pairs, one person pulling and one watching the wire and the post. In a Central Texas summer, start at first light and quit early.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What tools do I need to build a barbed wire fence?

At minimum: stakes and a string line, a manual t-post driver, a digging bar and post-hole digger or an auger for the corner posts, a wire unroller, a barbed wire stretcher, fencing pliers, staples and clips, heavy gloves and safety glasses. For the braces, add a saw, a drill with a long bit, and 9-gauge brace wire with a twist stick.

### What are fencing pliers used for?

Fencing pliers combine a hammer face for driving staples, a claw for pulling them, two wire cutters, gripping jaws for twisting and splicing, and a head you can roll against a post to take the slack out of a strand. They come out on every fence job, new build or repair. Buy forged steel with cutters hard enough for the wire you run.

### Can I stretch fence wire with a truck or tractor?

Not barbed or smooth wire. You cannot feel the tension through the machine, so it is easy to pull wire past its breaking point, and wire that breaks under load snaps back hard. Use a hand stretcher on barbed wire and ratchet in-line strainers on high-tensile wire. Net wire is sometimes pulled with a tractor on a stretcher bar, slowly and with someone watching the wire, but a come-along is the safer tool. Wear heavy gloves and eye protection every time.

### Should I rent or buy a post hole auger?

Rent it unless you set posts every year. A tractor or skid steer auger earns its keep past a few dozen holes, and in limestone or caliche you need a rock bit and enough machine to push it. For a few corner posts in decent soil, a digging bar and a clamshell digger do the job. In solid rock, get a crew's price before you book a week of rental.

### What should be in a fence repair kit?

Fencing pliers, a hand stretcher or a come-along with wire grips, splice sleeves and a crimper, a short roll of barbed wire, a coil of 9-gauge smooth wire, a few t-posts with a driver, clips and staples, heavy gloves, safety glasses, water and a first aid kit. Add a fence tester if you run electric wire. For bigger damage, see our [fence repair](/fence-repair-waco/) page.
