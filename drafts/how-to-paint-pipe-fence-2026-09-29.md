# How to Paint Pipe Fence So the Paint Outlasts the First Summer

To paint a pipe fence that holds up, get the oil off first, knock off everything loose (rust, mill scale, weld slag, flaking paint), prime the bare and rusty spots the same day, then put on two coats of an oil-based or direct-to-metal enamel when the steel is dry, above about 50 degrees, and well clear of the dew point. When pipe fence paint peels inside a year, the cause is usually prep or painting-day weather, not the brand in the can.

## Why pipe fence paint fails

Paint is a thin film. Anything between that film and sound steel becomes the weak point, and a pipe fence has plenty of candidates:

- **Oil, paraffin and thread dope.** Used drill stem, tubing and sucker rod spent their working life in a well and can reach the pipe yard with residue still on them, worst near the collars and ends.
- **Mill scale.** New hot-rolled pipe carries a blue-gray skin from the mill. Tight scale can be painted. Loose scale pops off in patches and takes the paint along.
- **Loose rust.** Tight surface rust is a workable base for a rusty-metal primer. Flaking, layered rust is already coming off.
- **Weld slag and spatter.** Every rail-to-post joint has a weld, and welds and cut ends are almost always where rust bleeds through first.
- **Dew and dust.** Paint over dew, or over a film of caliche dust off the county road, never bonds.

TxDOT's spec for painting steel bridges lists the same enemies: surfaces must be free of oil, grease, moisture, dirt, weld contamination, loose mill scale, rust and flaking paint before any coat goes on ([TxDOT Item 446, Field Cleaning and Painting Steel](https://ftp.txdot.gov/pub/txdot-info/cmd/cserve/specs/2024/standard/s446.pdf)). A fence is not a bridge, but the list is the same.

## Step 1: Degrease used oilfield pipe first

Do this before any brushing or grinding. Wire-wheel oily pipe and you just polish the oil into the steel. The TxDOT spec also puts solvent cleaning ahead of every other step.

Oil and paraffin show as dark, sticky patches that hold dust. Wipe them with mineral spirits on a rag, or scrub with a degreaser and a stiff brush, rinse, and let the steel dry completely. On a humid morning that can take until noon.

If you are building new, clean and prime the pipe on sawhorses before it is welded up. You can get all the way around every stick with no rails in the way.

## Step 2: Remove loose rust, scale and slag

Paint data sheets describe prep with coatings-industry standards (the SSPC numbers). Three matter on a fence:

| Prep level | What it means | Where it fits on a pipe fence |
|---|---|---|
| SSPC-SP 2, hand tool cleaning | Wire brush, scraper, sandpaper to remove loose rust, scale and flaking paint | Touch-ups, small pens, spot repairs |
| SSPC-SP 3, power tool cleaning | Wire wheel or flap disc on a grinder, same goal, faster | Most repaints and every weld |
| SSPC-SP 6, commercial blast | Abrasive blasting to mostly clean, profiled steel | Pipe blasted at a shop before welding, or a showpiece entrance |

Rust-Oleum's data sheet for its commercial alkyd primer calls for SP 2 or SP 3 cleaning to remove loose rust, mill scale and old coatings, and recommends blasting to SP 6 for best performance ([Rust-Oleum CM-08 technical data](https://www.rustoleum.com/-/media/DigitalEncyclopedia/Documents/RustoleumUSA/TDS/English/IBG/Commercial/Universal_Alkyd_Primer_CM08_GDH787_0621.ashx)). On a pasture fence, SP 3 with a knotted wire wheel on a 4.5-inch grinder is what most people actually do.

- **Hit the welds hardest.** Chip the slag, wire-wheel the bead and an inch or two either side, and knock off spatter. The bead is lumpy, so paint runs thin on its peaks.
- **Test peeled edges with a putty knife.** If the paint around a bare spot lifts easily, keep going until it doesn't, then feather the edge.
- **Do not chase tight rust to bright steel.** On used drill stem that is not realistic. The goal is a sound surface with nothing loose on it.
- **Treat old paint as possible lead.** Rust-Oleum's enamel sheet warns that sanding or scraping old paint can release lead dust and calls for a NIOSH-approved respirator. Wear one, and keep kids and stock away from the dust.

Blow the dust off before you prime. A leaf blower down the rails does most of it.

## Step 3: Pick the primer for the surface you have

Primer carries the rust protection. Topcoat is color and sun protection on top of it.

- **Rusty-metal primer.** Oil-based, made to go over tight, wire-brushed rust. The usual pick for used drill stem.
- **Clean-metal primer.** For new or blasted pipe. It does not bite into rust, so keep it off a brown fence.
- **Direct-to-metal (DTM) alkyd enamel.** An oil-modified enamel that can go straight onto prepared steel. Rust-Oleum's Professional DTM sheet lists a rusty-metal red primer and a clean-metal primer in the same family as its enamel colors, which keeps the coats compatible ([Rust-Oleum PRO-02 technical data](https://www.rustoleum.com/-/media/01F7F453EC2447689EF54859585C7B23.pdf)).
- **Rust converter.** For pitted spots you cannot grind clean. It turns the remaining rust into a paintable layer. Follow the label on wait time before topcoating.
- **Zinc-rich primer.** Zinc only protects when it touches bare steel, so save it for welds and cut ends you have ground bright. Check the label that your topcoat can go over it.

Both Rust-Oleum sheets say those alkyds are not for galvanized steel, and many others say the same. If any rails or gates are galvanized tubing, check the label before you buy.

Prime the same day you clean. Bare steel flash rusts overnight in Central Texas humidity.

## Step 4: Spray, roll, brush or mitt

Round pipe is awkward. A brush stroke covers one side, and the underside of a rail, where water sits, is where rust starts.

**Airless sprayer.** Fastest on long runs, but you are aiming at a target barely over 2 inches wide, so a lot of paint misses, and wind carries it onto trucks and barns. Spray on calm mornings, keep the gun 10 to 12 inches off the steel and moving (the PRO-02 sheet gives the same distance), and have a helper follow with a mitt.

**Pipe roller.** A curved roller wraps part of the rail, is quicker than a brush and wastes less than a sprayer. The PRO-02 sheet says rougher surfaces need a longer nap and a brush on edges and crevices.

**Paint mitt.** A lambswool glove you dip and wrap around the pipe. Slow, but it coats top and bottom in one pass with almost no waste. Best tool for rail undersides and around posts.

**Brush.** For welds. TxDOT has painters brush an extra stripe coat onto every edge and weld seam, carried at least an inch onto the flat steel, because a sprayed film runs thin there. The ranch version: dab primer into every weld and cut end, then prime the rest.

Two thin topcoats beat one heavy one, which sags on posts and stays soft under the skin. The PRO-02 enamel asks for 24 hours between coats. Primers vary, so read the can.

## How much paint a pipe fence takes

Work it out from the pipe. 2 3/8-inch pipe has about 0.62 square feet of surface per foot (3.14 x 2.375 inches around, x 12, divided by 144).

Say you have 1,000 feet of 3-rail fence, 2 3/8-inch rails and line posts on 8-foot centers, about 5 feet of post above ground:

- Rails: 3 x 1,000 feet x 0.62 = 1,860 square feet
- Posts: 125 x 5 feet x 0.62 = about 390 square feet
- Total: about 2,250 square feet per coat

The PRO-02 enamel lists 230 to 390 square feet per gallon at its recommended film thickness, allowing 15 percent loss, so roughly 6 to 10 gallons per topcoat. The CM-08 primer lists 200 to 250 square feet per gallon, so about 9 to 11 gallons to prime. Spraying round pipe loses more than 15 percent, and corners, gates and caps add more, so buy on the high side.

## When to paint: temperature, humidity and dew

The TxDOT spec allows no paint when steel or air is below 50 degrees F or will drop below 50 within 2 hours, on any surface with visible moisture, or when the steel is within 5 degrees of the dew point. Product sheets set their own windows: the CM-08 primer calls for 40 to 90 degrees and humidity under 85 percent, the PRO-02 enamel 32 to 90 degrees and humidity under 65 percent. Those limits apply to the steel surface as well as the air. Hold to the tighter number on the can you are using.

Around Waco that means:

- **Paint in spring and fall.** Mild days, air in the workable range.
- **Skip summer afternoons.** Waco routinely runs past 90 degrees in July and August, and dark pipe in full sun runs hotter than the air. Paint on hot steel dries before it levels.
- **Wait out the dew and quit early.** Pipe collects dew overnight even when the grass looks dry. Start when it is dry to your hand, and stop with enough daylight for the last section to set before evening dew.
- **Check the dew point.** Most phone weather apps show it. Steel cools faster than air overnight, so if the dew point is within about 5 degrees of the air temperature, the pipe can already be sweating.
- **Watch for a norther.** An overnight drop into the 40s, or a rain line, will ruin an uncured coat.

Keep stock off fresh paint. The CM-08 primer takes 4 to 7 days to reach full adhesion, so give it a few days before turning animals back in when you can.

## Color, bare pipe and rust

**Black and dark brown** hide rust bleed at welds and rub marks from cattle. **White** looks sharp on an entrance or [horse fence](/horse-fence-waco/) for a year or two, then shows every rust streak and mud splash. **Leaving it bare** is a fair choice on thick-wall drill stem in a working pen; our [used oilfield pipe](/blog/used-oilfield-pipe-fence/) article covers why many ranchers let it brown out. Pitting and thin wall are the real enemies, not surface color.

Two habits beat any color: cap every open post top so rain does not fill the post, and keep soil, brush and old wire from piling against the pipe and holding water.

## How often to repaint

There is no fixed number. It turns on prep, film thickness, sun and how hard stock uses the fence. A bull pen takes more rubbing in a year than frontage fence does in ten. Watch for:

- **Rust at welds and cut ends.** Wire-brush and spot-prime them every couple of years. Small job, and it pushes the big one out.
- **Chalking.** If your hand comes off the top rail colored, the topcoat is breaking down. Scrub the chalk off and let it dry before recoating, or the new coat bonds to powder instead of paint.
- **Peeling in sheets.** The first coat likely went over oil, scale or dew. Strip back to sound paint before recoating.

A sensible plan is touching up welds every few years and budgeting a full recoat somewhere around a decade in, in line with what our [pipe fence page](/pipe-fence-waco/) says about welded pipe.

## When paint is not the fix

Paint protects sound pipe. It does not put steel back. If a screwdriver goes through a rail, a weld has cracked, or posts have shifted in the clay and pulled the rails out of line, paint hides it for a season. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the best paint for pipe fence?
For most Central Texas pipe fence, an oil-based rusty-metal primer under two coats of oil-based or direct-to-metal alkyd enamel is the practical system. It tolerates wire-brushed rust, brushes and sprays easily, and stays in one product family so the coats bond. Epoxy is tougher but fussier. Check the label if any parts are galvanized.

### Do I need to prime used oilfield pipe before painting?
Yes, on any bare or rusty steel. Primer holds back rust and topcoat protects the primer from sun. Degrease first, remove loose rust, scale and slag, then prime the same day so the steel does not flash rust overnight. A DTM enamel can go direct to metal, but a rusty-metal primer does better on brown drill stem.

### Can you paint over rust on a pipe fence?
Over tight, well-bonded surface rust, yes, with a primer made for rusty metal. Over loose, flaking or layered rust, no. That rust is already letting go and will take the paint with it. Wire-wheel until nothing loose remains, blow off the dust, and treat pitted spots you cannot grind clean with a rust converter before priming.

### Should I spray or roll pipe fence paint?
Spray long runs and big pens on calm days, and expect to lose a lot of paint around round pipe. Roll or mitt smaller jobs, repaints and anything near vehicles. Combining them works best: brush primer into the welds, spray or roll the rails, then follow with a paint mitt on the undersides where water sits.

### What temperature is too cold to paint a pipe fence?
Read the can, since products differ. TxDOT's steel painting spec allows no paint below 50 degrees F, or when steel or air will drop below 50 within 2 hours, and requires the steel to be more than 5 degrees above the dew point. Some alkyd primers list 40 degrees as their floor, and some enamels go lower. Fifty degrees and dry steel is a safe rule for a fence.
