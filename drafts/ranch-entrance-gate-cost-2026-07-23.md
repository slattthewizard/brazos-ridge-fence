# Ranch Entrance Gate Cost: From Basic Pipe to Custom Iron

A ranch entrance gate can cost you $400 or $40,000, and both numbers are real. The gap between a plain tube gate hung on two posts and a custom iron entry with stone columns and a solar opener is enormous, and most landowners around Waco have no idea where their project falls on that line until the quotes come in.

This guide breaks down ranch entrance gate cost the way we quote it here in Central Texas. You'll see real prices for basic pipe gates, welded entrances, automated systems, and full custom iron builds, plus the factors that push a bid up or down. By the end you'll know what a fair number looks like before a contractor ever steps on your place.

## What a Ranch Entrance Gate Costs in Central Texas

Here's the short version. Most working ranch entrances in McLennan, Bosque, and Falls counties land between $1,200 and $8,500 installed. That covers a quality gate, two properly set posts or columns, and the labor to hang it so it swings true for decades.

Below that range you're looking at a farm-store tube gate on T-post hinges, which works fine for interior pastures but tends to sag at a main entrance that sees daily traffic. Above that range you're into automation, masonry columns, overhead signs, and custom ironwork, where $15,000 to $30,000 builds are common on larger properties along the Bosque River and out toward Crawford.

Three things set the price more than anything else:

- **Gate type and width.** A 12 foot single swing is cheap. A 20 foot double swing with a cantilever slide option is not.
- **What holds it up.** Two pipe posts in concrete might run $600. Two rock columns with lighting can run $9,000 by themselves.
- **Power and automation.** Openers, solar panels, keypads, and safety sensors add $2,500 to $7,000 to almost any entrance.

## Basic Pipe Gates: The Workhorse Option

For most ranches, a welded pipe gate is the right answer. It handles cattle pressure, survives a bump from the stock trailer, and doesn't rot. A basic pipe entrance around Waco typically runs:

- **12 foot single swing gate, installed:** $1,200 to $2,200
- **16 foot single swing, installed:** $1,600 to $2,800
- **20 foot double swing (two 10 foot leaves), installed:** $2,400 to $4,200

Those numbers include heavy-wall pipe posts set 3 to 4 feet deep in concrete, which matters more than the gate itself. A 16 foot gate hanging on a shallow post will drag the ground within two years in our clay soils. ## Custom Iron and Automated Ranch Entrances

This is where ranch entrance gate cost climbs fast, and where the money either shows every day or disappears into mistakes.

**Custom iron gates** run $6,000 to $15,000 installed for the gate itself, depending on size, design complexity, and finish. Plasma-cut silhouettes, your brand or ranch name worked into the panel, and powder coating over galvanizing all add cost. Powder coat over bare steel without proper prep is the classic corner-cut, and it'll be peeling in three Texas summers.

**Stone or brick columns** add $3,500 to $9,000 per pair around here, more if you want lighting inside them. Columns need real footings. We've torn out leaning columns that were set on 8 inches of concrete like a mailbox post.

**Automation** adds $2,500 to $7,000 depending on power. If grid power is near the entrance, you're at the low end. If the nearest outlet is 800 feet up the driveway, solar is usually cheaper than trenching wire, and modern solar openers handle a heavy iron gate fine.

Final invoice was $24,800. That sounds like a lot until you price what a comparable build runs in the Hill Country, where the same entrance quotes closer to $35,000.

On the smaller end, Karen Delgado in Valley Mills kept her existing 16 foot pipe gate and just added automation. Solar opener, keypad post, and a fire department access switch came to $3,900. Same daily convenience, fraction of the cost, because her gate and posts were already plumb and heavy enough to automate.

## What Drives Your Gate Price Up or Down

When two neighbors get quotes that differ by thousands for "the same gate," these factors usually explain it.

**Post depth and soil.** Central Texas clay swells and shrinks with moisture, and it will heave a shallow post out of plumb. Proper entrances get posts 3 to 4 feet deep with real concrete footings. The USDA Natural Resources Conservation Service has good soil survey data for our counties at [nrcs.usda.gov](https://www.nrcs.usda.gov/) if you want to see what you're building on. Hit rock instead of clay and drilling cost goes up, not down.

**Gate width.** County fire codes and common sense both favor wide entrances. A 16 foot minimum opening handles trucks and trailers. Go 20 feet if hay equipment or a gooseneck comes through regularly. Wider gates weigh more, need heavier posts, and cost more per foot than fence ever will.

**Setback from the road.** A gate set 30 to 50 feet off the county road lets you pull a truck and trailer fully off the pavement while the gate opens. That setback means wing fencing on both sides, which adds cost but prevents the daily hazard of a trailer hanging out in traffic.

**Power distance.** Every foot of trench for electrical service costs money. Past a few hundred feet, solar almost always wins.

**Matching fence.** An entrance rarely stands alone. If you're fencing the front of the property at the same time, combining the work saves mobilization cost. Our [ranch entrance gates](/ranch-entrance-gates-waco/) page covers full entrance packages, and if the whole perimeter is on the table, start with [what it costs to fence 10 acres](/blog/cost-to-fence-10-acres/) to frame the budget.

**Who builds it.** A welding shop can build a beautiful gate and still hang it wrong. A handyman can hang it cheap and have it dragging by fall. Texas A&M AgriLife Extension publishes solid fencing and ranch infrastructure guidance at [agrilifeextension.tamu.edu](https://agrilifeextension.tamu.edu/) that's worth reading before you compare bids.

## DIY or Hire It Out?

Plenty of ranchers can hang a gate, and we won't pretend otherwise. Here's an honest split.

**Reasonable DIY:** hanging a tube gate on existing sound posts, replacing hinges and latches, adding a wheel to support a long light gate, painting and greasing. If your posts are plumb and deep, swapping a gate is a Saturday job.

**Call a professional:** setting entrance posts in expansive clay or rock, hanging anything over 16 feet or over about 250 pounds, any masonry columns, and all automation. Heavy gates hurt people during installation, and a main entrance that fails blocks your only way in and out. If cattle are getting through a failed entrance onto a road, that's a liability problem on top of a fence problem, and it's exactly what our 24/7 emergency line exists for.

The math usually favors doing the main entrance right once. We see it constantly: a $900 self-install becomes a $2,400 repair three years later, which is more than the professional install would have cost on day one.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does a ranch entrance gate cost installed?

Most working ranch entrances in Central Texas run $1,200 to $4,200 installed for a quality welded pipe gate on properly set posts. Adding automation brings most projects to $4,000 to $10,000. Full custom iron entrances with stone columns typically run $15,000 to $30,000 or more depending on size and design.

### Is a custom iron entrance worth the money?

It depends on what the property is for. On a working cattle place, a heavy pipe gate does the same job for a fifth of the price. On a property you plan to sell, a hunting ranch, or a homestead where the entrance is the first thing every visitor sees, a custom entrance is one of the few improvements people notice from the road, and appraisers notice it too.

### How wide should my ranch entrance gate be?

Go 16 feet minimum for a main entrance, and 20 feet if you regularly bring in hay equipment, gooseneck trailers, or delivery trucks. Wider is cheaper to build now than to rebuild later. Also set the gate 30 to 50 feet back from the road so a truck and trailer can pull completely off the pavement while the gate opens.

### How long does gate installation take?

A basic pipe gate takes one day plus concrete cure time, so figure two short visits. Automated entrances usually take two to three days including electrical or solar setup and safety testing. Custom builds with masonry columns run one to three weeks depending on stonework and fabrication lead time, which is often the longest part of the wait.
